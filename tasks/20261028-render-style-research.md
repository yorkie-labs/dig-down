DIAGNOSIS
- 1. Two lighting models, and the hero is lit twice. The blocks use a hard 3-step MeshToonMaterial ramp, while the hero is a MeshLambertMaterial plane with smooth falloff. On top of that, the AI sprite already has highlights and shadows painted in, so the scene lights it a second time from a direction that doesn't match. The eye reads this as two different renderers. This is the main cause of the 'sticker between boxes' look (src/main3d.ts around line 270).
- 2. Texel density and dimensionality don't match. A dense HD illustration (or a pixel sprite minified with linear filtering and mipmaps) sits next to flat-colored cubes with a procedural frost texture. The drill is a real 3D primitive attached to a flat card, so its parallax shows the puppy is paper (main3d.ts lines 255-318).
- 3. The ink rules don't match. Block outlines are inverted hulls in world units, so their screen width changes with depth and perspective. The sprite's ink is baked in at whatever weight the AI picked, then softened by downscaling. Matching line weight is the strongest cohesion cue in flat-cartoon and pixel styles.
- 4. The camera projection fights the art. A near-frontal perspective camera shows side faces on the outer columns and pulls the shaft toward a vanishing point, next to a strictly front-view sprite. In pixel mode, perspective also makes stable pixels impossible: pixel creep can only be solved under orthographic projection (Texel Splatting / ProPixelizer).
- 5. A single front-view pose slides sideways and digs down. With no turn, dig, anticipation or landing frames, it moves like a cut-out pushed across a board, however good the shading is. Tidebreaker holds together largely because it has a full idle/walk/run/jump/hurt set made from one reference.
- 6. The hero isn't sitting inside the space. The plane floats in front of the carved cell, and a contact shadow is the only cue. The cavity has no dark interior faces, no occlusion, no rim light and no overlap from the block lip above, so the puppy reads as 'on the glass' rather than 'in the hole'.
- 7. There is no shared palette or grade. AI sprite colors, toon-lit block colors and fog each live in their own color world, and ACES tone mapping desaturates and shifts flat brand colors. Nothing at the end of the pipeline forces them onto one palette.
- 8. There is no value hierarchy or atmosphere layering. Sky, playfield and hero compete at similar contrast. There are no background or foreground layers, so the hero is the only thing on screen that looks drawn and looks foreign. The 'one warm key in a cold world' story that makes Tidebreaker cohere is missing.

UNIVERSAL
- Camera: switch to an OrthographicCamera, or a very narrow FOV (about 10-15 deg) pulled far back, with 0-8 deg tilt so block tops show a small frost lip. Required for pixel mode and good for every option.
- One shading rule for everything on screen. Regenerate the hero sprite as flat albedo with no painted lighting (at most one shadow tone), then light it with the same function as the blocks (shared toon ramp or self-lit plus rim). Draw the drill into the sprite frames; never attach a 3D primitive to a 2D card.
- One ink rule. Replace inverted hulls with a screen-space outline driven by chunk-ID and depth edges, at a constant pixel width. Merged same-color chunks then get one perimeter line and no inner seams. The hero writes its own ID to get the same line. This also removes the second instanced draw.
- Texel-density lock: fix pixels per cell (for example 16/24 for pixel, 48 for cartoon) and author the sprite, block textures and particles to it. Sprite textures use NearestFilter with no mipmaps in pixel styles, and the hero is about 1.2-1.5 cells tall.
- Grounding kit: sprite aligned to the camera plane and pivoted at the feet, alphaTest so it writes depth, a radial blob shadow that shrinks on hops, inset cavity wall meshes with darker ramp and vertex AO, and the front lip of the block above slightly overlapping the sprite.
- A minimum animation set before any style polish: idle (2f), side walk (4f), dig-side (3f), dig-down (3f), fall, land-squash, crushed, out-of-air. Play as a UV flipbook at 10-15 fps. Get it from the Spriterrific/ChatGPT reference sheet, or from the Dead Cells route (Tripo model, Blender rig, low-res frames plus normals).
- Gameplay-layer protection: the 9 playable columns are never blurred, fogged, dithered, bloomed or chromatically shifted. Atmosphere lives only in the sky, the walls, the background and empty space.
- Block palette discipline: 4 block colors per level with clearly separated luminance. Run a grayscale debug view as a CI-style check for every skin. Add a per-color glyph or face for redundant coding, keep block hues out of the environment palette, and offer a colorblind swap.
- Ambient floor: the block shader clamps max(lit, albedo * floor) so hues stay identifiable at any depth, plus a thin emissive edge so shapes read in the dark.
- Falling-hazard telegraph stack on top of position jitter: emissive pulse that speeds up, crack decal after 50%, frost-dust drip from the seam, and a danger shadow on the cells and hero below.
- Replace ACES with Neutral or NoToneMapping plus one final LUT/grade per 100 m depth band, interpolated on camera depth. Grade the environment harder than the blocks.
- Post budget: scene render (optional MRT for ID), at most one half- or quarter-res effect (bloom or lightmap), and ONE merged composite pass (outline, grade, vignette, grain, blue-noise dither). Add a 2-tier quality switch, cap pixelRatio, and default shadow mapSize to 512 on low tier.
- Juice kit with a settings toggle: trauma-squared camera shake (default 50%, never on the column the player reads), 2-4 frame hit-stop on big pops, block landing squash, and shard particles in the popped chunk's color.

CORRECTIONS:
- Diagnosis #4 / universal fixes: 'pixel creep can only be solved under orthographic projection' is contradicted by the pitch's own reference. The Texel Splatting paper (arXiv 2603.14587) is explicitly about perspective-stable 3D pixel art. Ortho is the simplest solution, not the only one.
- Diagnosis #3: inverted-hull outlines are not inherently world-unit. Extruding the hull in clip space (offset scaled by w) gives constant screen-pixel width. Also, in a near-flat ortho shaft, depth variation is negligible. The real flaws of the hull are inner seams between merged same-color cubes and the extra draw, not perspective width.
- Option D: a customDepthMaterial is unnecessary for alpha-cut cast shadows. WebGLShadowMap's default depth material inherits map/alphaMap/alphaTest from the source material. Note also that a PointLight shadow uses customDistanceMaterial, not customDepthMaterial; the current code sets customDepthMaterial on the sprite (src/main3d.ts:311), which matters if the headlamp casts shadows.
- Diagnosis #2 is confirmed and specific: pixel mode sets minFilter = NearestMipmapLinearFilter (src/main3d.ts:331), which still blends mip levels and softens the pixel sprite when minified. Use NearestFilter with generateMipmaps = false.
- Universal fixes: 'alphaTest so it writes depth' is slightly off. An opaque material writes depth anyway; alphaTest just discards transparent texels so they don't occlude. The real fix is to drop transparent: true on the sprite (currently transparent plus alphaTest 0.5 at line 270), so it sorts and writes depth as opaque.
- Option C: 'mobile_perf_risk: low' is understated. A pmndrs mip bloom plus a 1/4-res lightmap plus a full-screen merged effect plus a fake-SSS block shader is medium on mid-range Android at DPR 2+. B is the only option that is genuinely low.
- Option A: hue-shifted 2-tone toon on a sprite that was regenerated as 'flat-lit' re-introduces scene lighting of painted art (diagnosis #1). Either light it with the shared function and no fake puffy normal, or leave it unlit and add a rim, not both.
- Option B: 'snap-then-offset … vertical scroll makes this trivial' holds only for camera translation. The proposed 8-degree tilt needs the frustum-align approach from the three.js pixel example, and hero squash must still be quantized, as stated. That is fine, but it is not trivial with continuous wobble jitter on blocks, which must also be texel-snapped or it will shimmer.
- Option B MRT/ID: with WebGL2 MSAA plus MRT, the ID attachment must not be resolved or antialiased, or the edges get garbage IDs. At low internal res, use antialias: false everywhere, as stated. For A, at full res, this pitfall is real and unmentioned.
